## 1. Executive summary

**The local field is wide open.** Searching for lawn care in Rancho Cordova returns marketplaces (LawnStarter, Lawn Love, GreenPal, HomeAdvisor, HomeGuide) and directory listings, not owned brand websites. We found no website for JCP beyond its Google Business listing, and most nearby competitors only exist as directory entries. A genuinely premium site puts JCP in a different class from everyone it competes with locally.

**The best sites in the category fall into three camps,** and each leaves a gap:

1. *Enterprise commercial firms* are polished and credible but corporate and impersonal.
2. *Residential franchise brands* convert well but feel promotional, and their location pages are cloned templates.
3. *Marketplaces* win on instant pricing and review volume but sell interchangeable labor, not a crew you know.

None of them makes the actual craft visible: the stripes, the edges, the finish.

**Positioning:** *Lawns kept to a groundskeeper's standard.* Big-league consistency from a local crew, for homes and commercial properties alike.

**Signature experience:** a 3D lawn the visitor mows into stripes by scrolling, followed by an interactive pattern studio. Everything else is deliberately disciplined.

## 2. Method and limits

- **Benchmark selection:** Landscape Management's 2026 LM150 ranking of the largest US firms; national residential lawn brands; the marketplaces that rank in Rancho Cordova search results; the largest Canadian lawn and commercial operators; leading UK lawn-treatment and grounds-maintenance providers.
- **Facts** about rank, size, ownership and positioning come from public sources found during research (LM150 2026, company pages, directories).
- **Design observations** describe each brand's public positioning and the patterns typical of its category. Websites change often, so spot-check any specific detail before quoting it in sales material.
- This is a qualitative pattern study. No traffic or conversion data was available.

## 4. What the leaders have in common

1. **The quote is always one tap away.** Phone and quote buttons persist in the header and repeat after every major section.
2. **Audiences get separate paths.** Commercial buyers and homeowners have different jobs to be done, and the best sites split them early.
3. **Programs over one-off jobs.** Seasonal programs and recurring schedules are framed as the default.
4. **Proof stacks.** Reviews, accreditations, years in business and client logos sit close to calls to action.
5. **Local signals.** Even national franchises insist they are locally owned and know local conditions.
6. **Seasonal education.** Content about what the lawn needs this time of year builds authority and search traffic.
7. **Competitive pride.** "Best lawn on the street" messaging works on homeowners.

## 5. Where JCP can win

- **Make the craft visible.** Nobody shows the stripes and edges as the product. JCP's site does, literally, in 3D.
- **Be the local owned brand in a marketplace-dominated search.** A distinctive site plus a Google review engine beats anonymous listings.
- **Serve both audiences without diluting either.** One crew, two clear paths.
- **Own Valley-specific knowledge.** Hot, dry summers and wet winters are a story none of the national brands tells for Rancho Cordova.

## 6. Positioning and voice

**Line:** Lawns kept to a groundskeeper's standard.

**Pillars**
- *Precision:* clean lines, even stripes, crisp edges.
- *Reliability:* shows up on schedule, same finish every visit.
- *Local knowledge:* Sacramento Valley climate, grass types and seasons.
- *Two audiences, one standard:* homes and commercial properties.

**Voice:** plain, confident, specific. Active verbs, sentence case, no hype, no unverifiable superlatives.

## 7. Site structure (seven pages)

| Page | URL | Purpose | Key features |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home | `/` | Brand experience and routing to the right path | 3D mowing hero, statement, homes/commercial split, services track, visit checklist, process, tools teaser, reviews, quote band |
| Services | `/services` | Explain every service in depth | Sticky service index, eight detailed sections (how often, best time, good for, included, mini FAQ), per-service quote buttons |
| Residential | `/residential` | Convert homeowners | Four service rhythms with visit-cadence charts, six-step visit timeline, before/after slider, homeowner FAQ |
| Commercial | `/commercial` | Convert property managers | Six property types, six benefits, interactive scope builder that sends a draft scope with the quote, commercial onboarding process, manager FAQ |
| Lawn guide | `/lawn-guide` | Authority and local SEO | Lawn diagnostic, Sacramento Valley seasons, grass-type mowing-height ruler, four lawn rules, mowing pattern studio |
| Service area | `/service-area` | Local SEO and qualification | ZIP code checker, towns with ZIP codes, map, reviews |
| Contact | `/contact` | Capture the lead | Quote form pre-filled from any page, what happens next, full FAQ |

**Cross-page behavior:**
- Every "quote" button on any page opens the contact form with the property type, services and notes already filled in.
- Pages change through a striped "mowing" transition.
- Each page is written to its own HTML file at build time, with its own title, description and canonical URL.

**Still recommended for phase 2:**
- One page per city.
- A "Recent work" gallery with real before-and-after photos.
- Seasonal articles.

## 8. Design system

**Concept:** the visual language of grounds professionals, mowing stripes and top-down site plans.

**Palette**

| Token | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Turf | `#143A22` | Primary dark, text on light |
| Turf deep | `#0E2A18` | Darkest sections, footer |
| Fairway | `#2F6B3A` | Secondary green, labels |
| Stripe light / dark | `#8DB654` / `#4F7F35` | Stripes, illustrations |
| Chalk | `#F3F5EE` | Light backgrounds, text on dark |
| Valley gold | `#E4B842` | Calls to action, highlights |

**Type**
- *Big Shoulders Display:* tall, narrow strokes that echo grass blades and ballpark signage. Used for all display type.
- *Hanken Grotesk:* body and interface text.

**Illustration:** plan-view drawings (lot lines, footprints, striped lawns, trees as circles) that draw themselves in. No stock photography.

**Layout:** left-aligned, asymmetric two-column heads; generous whitespace. Numbering appears only on the four-step process, which is a real sequence.

## 9. Motion system

**Principle:** one orchestrated signature moment (the mowing hero). Everything else answers the visitor's scroll or click.

| Moment | Trigger | Technique |
|---|---|---|
| Preloader passes and wordmark | Page load | GSAP timeline |
| Hero headline word masks | After preloader | GSAP stagger |
| Lawn mowing, camera rise, statement | Scroll (pinned) | ScrollTrigger scrub driving shader uniforms |
| Grass parting around the cursor | Pointer | Raycast to ground plane, vertex shader push |
| Section headings | Enter viewport | Shared word-mask reveal |
| Statement words | Scroll | Scrubbed opacity |
| Marquee | Continuous; speeds up with scroll velocity | GSAP + ScrollTrigger velocity |
| Site plans | Enter viewport | CSS stroke-draw and stripe scale |
| Services track | Scroll (pinned) | Horizontal translate via ScrollTrigger |
| Pattern studio | Click | Canvas mower driven by a GSAP tween |
| Process rail | Scroll | Scrubbed scale |
| Season sun arc | Tab change | GSAP attribute tween on SVG path |
| Buttons | Hover | Label roll, magnetic pull |

Smooth scroll uses Lenis on GSAP's ticker. With `prefers-reduced-motion`, smooth scroll, pinning and scrubbing turn off, and the content shows in its final state.

## 10. Technology

- **Stack:** Vite, React 18, GSAP 3.13 with ScrollTrigger, Lenis, Three.js r169.
- **Lawn scene:** about 95,000 instanced blades on desktop (34,000 on small or low-power devices).
  - A custom shader handles wind, the mowing line, stripe shading, clippings and the golden-hour sky dome.
  - The scene loads as a separate chunk after first paint.
  - It pauses when off screen.
  - It falls back to a painted CSS lawn if WebGL is unavailable.
- **Accessibility:**
  - Skip link and keyboard-operable tabs, accordion and menu.
  - Visible focus states.
  - Labeled form fields with specific error messages.
  - Reduced-motion support.
- **Performance budget:**
  - First paint does not wait for Three.js.
  - Device pixel ratio capped at 1.75.
  - No images to download.
  - Fonts loaded with `display=swap`.

## 11. Local SEO plan

1. **Google Business Profile:**
   - Choose categories carefully.
   - Add service areas matching the site.
   - Post weekly photos.
   - Reply to every review.
2. **Review engine:** after each first service, text a direct review link (the "Read our Google reviews" button on the site uses the listing).
3. **Name, address, phone consistency:** use exactly the same details on Yelp, Nextdoor, Angi, BBB and Thumbtack.
4. **Structured data:** LocalBusiness schema is already in `index.html`; update it if hours, licensing or services change.
5. **City pages (phase 2):** genuinely local content per town, not cloned templates.
6. **Seasonal articles:** for example, "When to aerate a Sacramento lawn" or "Summer mowing height in the Valley."
